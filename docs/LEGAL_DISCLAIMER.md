### 2. Execution and Attestation Requirements (Section 63, Indian Succession Act, 1925)
To ensure the legal validity of any testamentary document generated through this platform:
1. **Mental Capacity:** The Testator must execute the Will while being of sound disposing mind, memory, and understanding, free from coercion, intoxication, fraud, or undue influence.
2. **Two Independent Witnesses:** The Testator must sign or affix their mark in the presence of at least **two (2) adult witnesses** present at the same time.
3. **No Beneficiary as Witness:** Under Section 67 of the Indian Succession Act, 1925, any bequest made to an attesting witness or the spouse of an attesting witness is **VOID**. Witnesses must be independent, disinterested parties (e.g., trusted family doctors, chartered accountants, or longstanding neighbors).

### 3. Statutory Clarification on Bank Nominees vs. Legal Heirs
Users are explicitly cautioned regarding the landmark precedents established by the Supreme Court of India in:
- *Smt. Sarbati Devi and Anr. v. Smt. Usha Devi* (1984 AIR 346, 1984 SCR (1) 992)
- *Shakti Yezdani & Anr. v. Jayanand Jayant Salgaonkar & Ors.* (Supreme Court of India, Civil Appeal No. 7111 of 2013, Decided Dec 14, 2023)

> **Statutory Rule:** A Nominee designated in bank accounts, fixed deposits, demat shares, mutual funds, provident funds, or insurance policies is merely a **custodian/trustee** authorized to receive proceeds from the financial institution upon the holder's demise. The nomination **does not** confer beneficial ownership or title. The proceeds form part of the deceased's general estate and must devolve upon the legal heirs designated in the registered Will or governed by intestate succession laws.

### 4. Equal Rights of Daughters (Hindu Succession Amendment Act, 2005)
As confirmed by the Supreme Court of India in *Vineeta Sharma v. Rakesh Sharma* ((2020) 9 SCC 1):
- A daughter is recognized as a coparcener by birth with equal rights, liabilities, and status as that of a son in ancestral Joint Hindu Family Property.
- Daughters married prior to 2005 enjoy equal coparcenary birthrights. Any testamentary attempt to disinherit daughters from ancestral coparcenary property without a registered relinquishment deed (*Haq-Tyag*) or formal family partition deed may be contested in a court of competent jurisdiction.

---

### 5. Partnership Business Succession (Section 42, Indian Partnership Act, 1932)
Unless a commercial partnership deed contains an explicit contract to the contrary, a partnership firm stands **dissolved** upon the death of any partner. Bequests of partnership shares must be corroborated with an executed Partnership Addendum or fresh Partnership Deed admitted by the surviving partners.

---

### 6. Sub-Registrar Registration Notice
Registration of a Will is **optional** under Section 18 of the Indian Registration Act, 1908. An unregistered Will written on plain paper and duly witnessed is legally enforceable. However, formal registration at the Sub-Registrar Office having jurisdiction over the testator's residence is strongly recommended for business owners and high-net-worth joint families to establish prima facie genuineness and preclude future challenges alleging forgery or testamentary incapacity.
